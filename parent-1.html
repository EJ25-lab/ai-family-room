🧰 Parent Intel Track · Mission P-1 ~8 min

The Accommodation Translator.

You shouldn't need a law degree to understand your kid's plan. AI translates the dense paragraphs into "what this means for my Tuesday." Then helps you write the warm, specific partnership email that gets a response.

⚡ +50 XP 📝 Translator Badge 🔧 Claude
A note before you start

Your child's IEP or 504 is a FERPA-protected record. It belongs to your family. The good news: the mission below works from your understanding of the document, not the document itself. The protected plan stays where it belongs — with you. Claude only needs the words you want translated and a sentence in your own language.

1
Sit with the section. Find the words you want translated.

Open the document on your end — just you and the paper. Find the 3 hardest words or phrases in it. Those are what we're translating. Your document stays with you.

🧠 Open Claude.ai →
2
Ask Claude to translate it.

Paste this prompt before your section. Replace the part in brackets with the language from your document.

Copy & paste · then drop your section underneath

I am a parent. I want to understand my kid's school plan in plain language so I can advocate for what they actually need.

Please translate the terms and phrases below into:
(1) What this means in plain English — like you're explaining it to a friend.
(2) What it should look like on a regular Tuesday at school.
(3) What I should ask the school to confirm.

The terms I'm trying to understand are: [type the specific words or phrases]. Here's the situation in my own words, no names: [type 2–3 sentences about what you've noticed for your kid].

3
Ask Claude to draft the partnership email.

Now turn the translation into the email. Warm, specific, and partnership-oriented. The teacher or case manager is your partner, not your opponent.

Copy & paste · uses your translation from step 2

Based on the translation we just made, please write a short partnership email I can send to the case manager.

Tone: warm, specific, partnership-oriented. I know they are juggling a lot.
Goal: name what I noticed, name what the plan says, ask one specific thing — what I'm hoping for on a regular school day.

Keep it under 150 words. Sign-off line as "a parent." I'll add the real names myself when I'm ready to send.

You translated it. You wrote the email.
