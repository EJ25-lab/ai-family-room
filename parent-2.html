🧰 Parent Intel Track · Mission P-2 ~10 min

The Hard Conversation Prep.

For the meeting you've been dreading. The pediatrician. The counselor. The principal. AI helps you organize what you've been noticing into a clean one-pager you bring with you. You don't walk in cold. You walk in prepared.

⚡ +50 XP 💬 In the Room Badge 🔧 Claude
A note before you brain dump

Claude can help you organize what you've been noticing without knowing your kid's name, the doctor's name, or specifics that belong to your family. "My child" and "a regular meeting" is plenty for the prep. Save the real names for the room you're walking into — that's where they matter.

1
Open Claude. Tell it what you've been noticing — in general terms.

No structure required. Brain dump. The thing keeping you up. The pattern you keep seeing. The moments that made you say "that's not okay." Voice-to-text works. Skip the names — use "my child" and "the meeting."

🧠 Open Claude.ai →
2
Ask Claude to organize it into a one-page conversation map.

Paste this prompt before your brain dump. Replace the bracketed parts with your details.

Copy & paste · then drop your brain dump underneath

I am a parent preparing for a meeting about my child. The meeting is with a [provider type — no name]. I have [how many] minutes.

Please take everything I share below and organize it into a one-page conversation map with three sections:
1. What I've noticed — the patterns and moments, in plain language.
2. What concerns me — why this matters, what I'm worried about long-term.
3. What I'm asking for — one or two specific asks (an evaluation, a meeting, a referral, a trial of something).

Keep it warm and partnership-oriented. I am not here to fight. I am here to be heard, and to make sure my child is.

Here is everything I've been noticing — in my own language, with no identifying details: [type your brain dump without names, schools, or providers]

3
Save it. Print it. Bring it with you.

This is your one-pager. Fold it in your bag. Glance at it when you sit down. You don't have to remember — you have it written down. That is preparation, not nerves.

Optional follow-up · only if you want to rehearse

Now please write a 2-minute version of this I can practice saying out loud. Same three sections, same warmth, but shortened to what I'd say if they only had two minutes for me.

End with one specific question I should ask before I leave.

You walked in prepared.
