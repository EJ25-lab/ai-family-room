# AI Family Room — Brand Voice

**Internal reference.** Not published to the live site. Every line of new copy on the site should pass these checks before shipping.

---

## The four principles

### 1 · Grace first

Assume the person on the other end is overloaded, not adversarial. The teacher juggling 90 kids. The doctor whose inbox is buried. The VA caseworker with 200 files. The landlord who hasn't checked email in days. The IEP team who's working hard inside a broken system.

**Never write copy that positions the user as a victim of someone else's malice.** That framing wins nothing and burns relationships our community needs.

If a line implies "they ignored you" or "make them pay attention" or "they finally have to answer," rewrite it.

### 2 · You stayed in the room

The user's win is **showing up well**, not winning an argument. The follow-up email isn't about getting even — it's about keeping the partnership alive when life gets loud.

Mission completions celebrate the user's character, not their leverage. "You stayed in the room" lands harder than "You got them to answer."

### 3 · Partners, not opponents

Teachers, doctors, FRG leads, landlords, IEP teams, school principals, base coordinators — they are all people doing their best in broken systems. The site treats them as collaborators in the user's life, not obstacles to be defeated.

When in doubt, ask: would a teacher reading this feel respected? If no, rewrite.

### 4 · Warm + specific beats firm + impatient

The brand voice for tough conversations:
- **Warm** — acknowledge the other person's reality before stating yours
- **Specific** — name what's slipping, propose a concrete next step
- **Partnership-oriented** — invite the other person to build the solution with you
- **Bounded** — clear about what you need without being adversarial

What we never sound like:
- Petty
- Vindictive
- Cynical about institutions
- Snarky toward educators or public servants
- "Karen energy" toward customer service

## Quick rewrite table

| Avoid this | Reach for this |
|---|---|
| "the teacher who never replied" | "the teacher juggling 90 kids whose inbox got away from her" |
| "they finally have to answer" | "you stayed in the room" |
| "make them pay attention" | "invite them back to the conversation" |
| "the email that gets a teacher to actually reply" | "the email that opens the door" |
| "silence is not consent" | "the follow-up that keeps the partnership alive" |
| "turn silence into accountability" | "kept the conversation going when it would have been easier to let it drop" |
| "the school to actually do what they said" | "a clear system that protects my kid without overloading the team" |
| "ignored your request" | "didn't get back to you yet" |
| "ghosting you" | "buried in their inbox" |
| "the doctor who ghosted" | "the doctor's office where messages stack up" |

## Tone-by-track

**Foundations track** — Discovery energy. Permission to be messy. "You already had the voice. AI just turned up the volume."

**Build Your Superpower track** — Forward motion. Practical wins. "From your head to a real document, in minutes."

**Mil-Spouse Intel track** — Operational seriousness. Grace under pressure. Acknowledge OPSEC without paranoia. "Built for the life you actually live."

## Tests before shipping copy

1. **The Tyra test** — would Tyra say this line out loud to a parent at a Petersburg parent night? If it sounds like a marketing line, rewrite.
2. **The teacher test** — would an educator reading this feel respected?
3. **The grace test** — does this position someone else as the villain? If yes, rewrite.
4. **The mom test** — does this sound like a thoughtful mom-friend who's been through it? If it sounds like a startup, rewrite.
