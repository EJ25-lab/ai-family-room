USE IT WELL

What could
go wrong.

Four real 2026 AI failures. Four plain rules. A clear answer on which tool to use when. So you can use AI — and not end up in a story like one of these.

AI is a tool. Tools have rules. These four stories happened in 2026, mostly in the last six months. They are not edge cases — they are the pattern of what happens when the rules get skipped. Each one points to one thing you can do differently.

FOUR CASES · FOUR RULES

What's actually happening.

Plain reporting. Real sources. Read once and you will spot the same pattern when it shows up in your inbox.

1
Verify anything that matters
Q1 2026 · U.S. federal courts

$145,000+ in sanctions for AI-fabricated case law.

In the first quarter of 2026 alone, U.S. judges imposed over $145,000 in sanctions on lawyers who submitted briefs full of citations that AI invented. A top-ranked attorney at Sullivan & Cromwell filed a motion with more than 40 hallucinated errors. U.S. District Judge Kai N. Scott sanctioned one attorney $5,000 — after sanctioning the same attorney $2,500 earlier for the same problem. A public database now tracks over 1,353 documented cases globally, and the pace is accelerating.

The takeaway

Experts — people who spent decades learning a field — are still being fooled by AI that confidently invents facts. If you're using AI to write a complaint, an advocacy letter, or anything you'll cite: verify every claim before you send.

Sources: CNN Business · ComplianceHub · Charlotin Database

2
Protect what you can't take back
2026 · FBI · AARP

$2.3B lost to AI voice clones — from three seconds of audio.

In 2026, 1 in 4 Americans received a deepfake voice call in the past 12 months. AI voice-cloning scams have cost elderly Americans over $2.3 billion this year alone. Scammers need only three seconds of audio — from a TikTok, an Instagram Reel, a Facebook video — to clone the voice of your kid, your grandmother, your spouse. 77% of people who engage with the call end up losing money, typically between $500 and $15,000. The recommended defense: a family safe word that only your household knows.

The takeaway

Never paste private info into AI tools you wouldn't put in an email. Don't post family voice clips publicly if you can avoid it. Set a family safe word today. Use it on any urgent call before you act.

Sources: SavingAdvice / Hiya State of the Call 2026 · BBB / WIS-TV · KCTV5

3
Draft, not verdict
2025–2026 · UnitedHealth · multi-state

An AI that cut off care — with a 90% error rate.

Multiple 2026 lawsuits allege UnitedHealth's nH Predict AI system — used to decide when to stop covering patients in rehabilitation centers — has a 90% error rate when patients appeal. Medicare Advantage denials are overturned on appeal 82% of the time. By spring 2026, Arizona, Maryland, Nebraska, and Texas had banned AI as the sole decisionmaker in insurance denials. North Carolina HB 565 followed.

The takeaway

A multibillion-dollar company treated AI output as a verdict. The cost landed on patients in real beds. Treat AI as a draft — always. If an institution uses AI to deny you something, appeal. The reversal rate says you're probably right.

Sources: Stanford Report · Stateline · WRAL

4
Teach the kids — don't hide it
2026 · 90+ U.S. schools

Deepfake nude images of classmates in 90+ schools.

By spring 2026, Wired had documented AI-generated deepfake nude images of students in at least 90 U.S. schools. Documented incidents include Radnor High (PA), Council Rock School District (PA), Lancaster Country Day School (PA) — where two 14-year-old boys admitted creating 59 child sexual abuse images of their classmates — and Hingham Middle School (MA). A new federal law passed in May 2026. Schools are now removing student photos from public websites. It is typically teenage boys creating the images of female classmates.

The takeaway

Kids will find AI. They will use it. The choice is whether they learn responsibly in front of an adult — or alone, in secret. Talk to them this week. About what AI can do. About what they should never do with it. About what to do if it's done to them. The conversation costs nothing. The silence costs a lot.

Sources: 404 Media · Boston Globe · KOLD News · Malwarebytes

🌱 THE FIRST PRINCIPLE

Bad prompts give bad output. Good prompts respect what the AI can do.

Most of the cases above share one root cause: the human treated AI like a colleague who already knew everything. AI is not that. AI is a brilliant intern who has read millions of books, will confidently answer anything you ask, and has no idea what it doesn't know. Your job is to ask carefully — and to verify.

That skill is called prompting. It's not magic. It's communication. The room teaches it in five minutes.

🛠 THE TOOLBOX

Pick the right tool.

Most people use one tool for everything and get frustrated. Different tools are good at different jobs. Here is the short answer:

🧠
Claude · Anthropic

Best for: Conversations, advocacy emails, hard-conversation prep, long writing, working through a problem.

Skip when: you need live web search or today's news.

Free tier · Pro $20/mo

🔍
Perplexity · Perplexity AI

Best for: Research with citations, current information, sourcing facts.

Skip when: you're writing something personal — Claude is warmer.

Free · Pro $20/mo

🎨
Gamma · Gamma

Best for: Slide decks, one-pagers, visual documents from one sentence.

Skip when: a plain email or memo would do the job.

400 credits free · Plus $8–$10/mo

✏️
NeuralWriter · warm-up

Best for: Turning a rough thought into a clear prompt before Claude.

Skip when: you've gotten comfortable writing prompts yourself.

10K chars free · From $19/mo

New tools come out constantly. We update this page as the landscape shifts. The monthly Field Notes from The Collective will tell you when something better shows up.

📦 ONE TOOL, MANY JOBS

When one tool can cover most of it.

If you want to learn one tool and stop:

  • 🧠 Claude (Anthropic) — strongest single tool for most family and personal use. Conversations + writing + light research + analysis. This is the one the room is built around.
  • 💬 ChatGPT (OpenAI) — strong alternative. Image generation built in. Widely known. Free tier exists.
  • 📊 Copilot (Microsoft 365) — bundled with Microsoft 365 if you already pay for Office. Useful for Word/Excel/Outlook work.

If you only learn one, learn Claude. It's the one this room is built around. When you outgrow it — you'll know.

Now use it well.

You have the rules. You have the toolbox. You know which stories not to be in.
